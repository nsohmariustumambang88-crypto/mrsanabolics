## What Makes a Steroid "Good" for Strength?

**Strength vs. Muscle is different.**

A steroid that builds 30 lbs muscle might not double your bench press. Conversely, some steroids dramatically increase strength with modest muscle gain. The difference lies in:

1. **CNS Stimulation** — direct nerve-muscle connection improvement
2. **Myonuclei Addition** — more cell nuclei = faster force production
3. **IGF-1 & HGH** — growth factors that increase power
4. **Intramuscular Coordination** — muscles recruiting more fibers simultaneously
5. **Aggression/Drive** — psychological push to train harder

## Training for Maximum Strength on Steroids

**Strength training principles don't change on steroids — they just matter more.**

### Low Reps, Heavy Weight
- Bench: 3-5 reps per set
- Squat: 3-5 reps per set
- Deadlift: 1-5 reps per set
- Rows: 4-6 reps per set

**Why:** Strength is trained at 85%+ of 1RM. Higher reps build muscle but not maximum strength.

### Progressive Overload
- Week 1: 405 lbs bench for 5 reps
- Week 2: 410 lbs bench for 5 reps
- Week 3: 415 lbs bench for 4-5 reps
- Week 4: Deload, 380 lbs for 5 reps (recovery week)

**Principle:** Add 2.5-5 lbs per week on major lifts. Steroids support this progression.

### Reduced Volume
- Powerlifters do 8-12 total sets per exercise
- Bodybuilders do 12-20 total sets per exercise

**Why:** High volume causes overuse injuries. Strength athletes need recovery between sessions.

## Strength Gains vs. Muscle Gain

**100 lb Bench Increase ≠ 30 lb Muscle Gain**

Strength comes from:
- Neurological adaptation (CNS learning to recruit more fibers) — 30% of gain
- Muscle gain (actual tissue growth) — 50% of gain
- Intramuscular water/glycogen — 20% of gain

A lifter can gain 100 lbs on bench with only 8-10 lbs actual muscle gain. This is why strength cycles feel drastic but don't always look huge.

## Strength Cycle Duration

**Strength cycles are typically shorter than muscle cycles.**

- **Muscle cycle:** 12 weeks (body needs time to add tissue)
- **Strength cycle:** 8-10 weeks (nervous system adapts faster)
- **Peaking cycle:** 4 weeks (Halotestin, pre-competition only)

Strength plateaus faster than muscle growth. A 16-week strength cycle sees most gains in weeks 1-8, then minimal gains weeks 9-16.

---

## Post-Cycle Strength Loss

**After a strength cycle ends, you lose some strength.**

- **Muscle gained:** 70-80% is retained post-cycle
- **Strength gained:** 40-60% is retained post-cycle

A lifter who goes from 405 bench to 505 (100 lb gain) will likely drop back to 445-465 post-cycle (45-60 lb gain retained).

This is why strength athletes run cycles periodically — to break through strength plateaus every 3-4 months.
