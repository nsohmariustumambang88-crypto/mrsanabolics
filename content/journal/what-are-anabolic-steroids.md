Anabolic steroids are man-made relatives of testosterone, and they work by switching on the same receptor that testosterone does. This article explains what they are, how they act in the body, what doctors prescribe them for, and how they differ from the corticosteroids used for asthma or eczema, with the law and the health risks in brief. Every factual claim is linked to its source. It contains nothing about doses, cycles or what to take.

> **The short answer**
> - Anabolic steroids are synthetic substances similar to the male sex hormone testosterone.[^1]
> - They work mainly by binding to and activating the androgen receptor, which switches on genes in muscle and other tissues.[^2][^3]
> - A few are approved medicines, for conditions such as testosterone deficiency and delayed puberty.[^2]
> - They are not the same as corticosteroids, the anti-inflammatory medicines used for asthma or eczema.[^4]

## What are anabolic steroids?

The US National Institute on Drug Abuse (NIDA) defines them as "synthetic substances similar to the male sex hormone testosterone". Their full name is anabolic-androgenic steroids, often shortened to AAS, anabolic steroids, or simply steroids.[^1]

The US Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) gives the chemistry in one sentence: "Most anabolic steroids are synthetic derivatives of testosterone, and their chemical properties are defined by their core four-ring structure and the various chemical modifications."[^2] Some are taken by mouth, some are injected into muscle, and some come as gels or creams applied to the skin.[^1]

US federal law uses a wider definition: "any drug or hormonal substance, chemically and pharmacologically related to testosterone (other than estrogens, progestins, corticosteroids, and dehydroepiandrosterone)".[^5]

Testosterone was isolated and synthesised in the 1930s.[^6] According to a scientific statement from the Endocrine Society, widespread illicit use of performance-enhancing drugs did not appear in the general population until the 1980s and 1990s, and today "the vast majority of PED users are not athletes but rather nonathlete weightlifters".[^6] A 2014 meta-analysis of 187 studies estimated the global lifetime prevalence of anabolic steroid use at 3.3%: 6.4% for men and 1.6% for women.[^7]

## Anabolic and androgenic: what the name means

The two halves of the name describe two kinds of effect. NIDA says anabolic steroids promote "the growth of skeletal muscle (anabolic effects)" and "the development of male sexual characteristics (androgenic effects) in both males and females".[^1] The DEA adds bone density and red blood cell production (erythropoiesis) to the anabolic side.[^2]

Most synthetic steroids were made by altering the testosterone molecule to "enhance the protein anabolic effect relative to the androgenic effect". A 2008 review of their pharmacology in the *British Journal of Pharmacology* is clear about how far chemists got: "complete dissociation has not been achieved with any anabolic steroid synthesized".[^3] Every anabolic steroid is also androgenic.

> **About "anabolic to androgenic ratios"**
> The ratios quoted online for individual steroids trace back to assays in rats, which compared the growth of a muscle called the levator ani with the growth of sex organs. The same review records decades of criticism of the method, including findings that the levator ani "reflects a general genitomyotrophic response rather than an overall response to androgens", alongside biochemical evidence that it may still indicate general muscle response in the developing rat.[^3] Either way, the figures come from animal experiments and should be read that way.

## How do steroids work?

### The androgen receptor

"Anabolic steroids bind to and activate androgen receptors in the body," the DEA says. Activation of those receptors in different cells and tissues primarily mediates both the anabolic and the androgenic effects.[^2] The Endocrine Society statement says the same of muscle: "Most of the anabolic effects of testosterone appear to be mediated through androgen receptor signaling."[^6]

The pharmacology review describes what happens inside the cell:[^3]

1. The steroid binds to the receptor, which separates the receptor from a heat shock protein (Hsp90) it is bound to.
2. The activated receptor moves into the cell's nucleus.
3. Pairs of receptors attach to steroid response elements on the DNA.
4. That triggers the formation of a transcription complex, which switches on the target genes.

In other words, a steroid changes which genes a cell reads, and so which proteins it makes.

### Why one steroid differs from another

Some steroids act more on muscle relative to sexual tissue than others, though never entirely. The review says differences in how much androgen receptor a tissue expresses appear to be key to this partial separation, together with how the steroid is metabolised inside the cell and how the bound receptor interacts with partner proteins called co-activators.[^3] Metabolism is the easiest of these to see:

- **Conversion to DHT.** In reproductive tissues, testosterone is readily converted by the enzyme 5α-reductase into dihydrotestosterone (DHT), a more potent androgen. In skeletal muscle, "5α-reductase activity is negligible". The review uses this to explain why nandrolone (19-nortestosterone) scores a higher muscle-to-androgenic ratio than testosterone in those animal assays.[^3]
- **Conversion to oestrogen.** Testosterone is also converted by the enzyme aromatase into the oestrogen oestradiol.[^3]
- **Steroids taken by mouth.** Oral activity can be conferred by a chemical change at the 17α position of the molecule (17α-alkylation), which "prevents deactivation of the steroid by first-pass metabolism". The review associates liver toxicity with these 17α-alkylated, orally active steroids.[^3]
- **Injectable esters.** For injection, the molecule is usually joined to an acid (esterified) "to prevent rapid absorption from the oily vehicle". Enzymes in the blood then remove the ester to release the active steroid, and "the longer the chain length, the more slowly the preparation is released into circulation".[^3] The second word in names such as [testosterone enanthate](/shop/injectables/testosterone-enanthate), testosterone cypionate or [nandrolone](/shop/injectables/nandrolone) decanoate is the ester.[^3]

### Mechanisms that are still uncertain

Two further ideas remain open. One is that steroids also protect muscle from breakdown by interfering with the glucocorticoid hormone system. The review calls this "an attractive hypothesis", while noting that most binding studies show very low binding affinity of anabolic steroids for the glucocorticoid receptor. The other is "non-genomic" signalling, which does not work through the genes and can start within seconds to a few minutes. The review says it is not currently known whether these actions of androgens at physiological concentrations are important in skeletal muscle growth.[^3]

### What a controlled trial showed

In a randomised, placebo-controlled trial published in the *New England Journal of Medicine* in 1996, 43 healthy men received either testosterone at doses above the normal physiological range or a placebo, with or without strength training. The authors concluded that supraphysiologic testosterone, "especially when combined with strength training", increases "fat-free mass and muscle size and strength in normal men".[^8] The trial was small and short, and it measured muscle and strength, not long-term health. For that, see [the health risks of anabolic steroids](/blog/anabolic-steroid-health-risks).

### What happens to the body's own testosterone

Steroids taken from outside the body suppress the body's own production. The Endocrine Society statement explains: "Because exogenous AAS suppresses hypothalamic-pituitary-testicular (HPT) function, users will gradually develop suppressed testosterone levels and may become hypogonadal upon discontinuation of AAS use." It describes fatigue, loss of libido and depression after stopping, which may lead some people to start again, and links this to dependence.[^6] NIDA cites a review estimating that about 32 percent of people who misuse anabolic steroids become dependent.[^1] What studies found about recovery is set out in [post-cycle therapy: what the research says](/blog/post-cycle-therapy-complete-guide).

## What are anabolic steroids used for in medicine?

NIDA says they are sometimes used to treat delayed puberty and muscle loss due to disease, and low testosterone in men with an associated medical condition. They "can also improve feelings of well-being and increase bone strength", NIDA adds, "but are not approved for these purposes".[^1]

In the United States, the DEA says, only a small number are approved for human or animal use. It names testosterone and several of its esters, methyltestosterone, nandrolone decanoate and oxandrolone as the main ones prescribed. The approved uses it lists include testosterone deficiency, delayed puberty, anaemia, breast cancer and AIDS-related tissue wasting. Three more, trenbolone, boldenone and mibolerone, are used only in veterinary medicine.[^2]

A drug label shows how narrow an approval is. The US label for testosterone enanthate injection covers replacement therapy where the body's own testosterone is deficient or absent (primary hypogonadism and hypogonadotropic hypogonadism), stimulating puberty "in carefully selected males with clearly delayed puberty", and secondary use in some women with advanced, inoperable breast cancer that has spread to the bone. It states that safety and efficacy "in men with age-related hypogonadism have not been established".[^9]

Approvals can also end. In June 2023 the FDA withdrew approval of oxandrolone tablets. After reviewing the data on their safety and effectiveness, it said it believed "the potential problems associated with oxandrolone tablets are sufficiently serious" that the drug should be removed from the market. The problems it cited from the labelling included peliosis hepatis, "sometimes associated with liver failure and intra-abdominal hemorrhage", liver cell tumours, and blood lipid changes associated with an increased risk of atherosclerosis.[^10]

## Anabolic steroids vs corticosteroids

"Steroid" covers two very different groups of medicines. The ones a doctor prescribes for asthma, eczema or rheumatoid arthritis are corticosteroids, and the NHS is direct about the difference: "These types of steroids are not the same as anabolic steroids, which some people use illegally to help build bigger muscles."[^4]

| | Anabolic steroids | Corticosteroids |
|---|---|---|
| What they are | Synthetic substances similar to testosterone[^1] | Artificial versions of hormones the body makes by itself[^4] |
| What they act on | The androgen receptor[^2] | Inflammation and the immune system: they reduce redness and swelling and stop the immune system attacking itself[^4] |
| Medical uses | Low testosterone with an associated medical condition, delayed puberty, muscle loss due to disease[^1] | Conditions including asthma, allergies, eczema, rheumatoid arthritis and Crohn's disease[^4] |
| US Controlled Substances Act | Schedule III[^2] | Excluded from the legal definition of "anabolic steroid"[^5] |
| WADA Prohibited List 2026 | Section S1, prohibited at all times[^11] | Section S9 (glucocorticoids), prohibited in competition when given by injection, by mouth or rectally[^11] |

How sport treats each group is explained in [the WADA Prohibited List explained](/blog/wada-banned-list-explained).

## Are anabolic steroids legal?

It depends on the country, and on whether you possess, import or supply them. In the United States, Congress has placed a total of 64 anabolic steroids in Schedule III of the Controlled Substances Act,[^2] and the legal definition reaches related substances that are not named.[^5] In the UK, the NHS describes them as "class C medicines, which can only be issued by pharmacists with a prescription", and says supplying them carries up to 14 years in prison.[^12]

The detail matters, particularly for buying online and importing. It is set out, quoted from the legislation, in [anabolic steroids and the law in the US, UK, Canada and Australia](/blog/anabolic-steroids-law-us-uk-canada-australia). SARMs are regulated differently: see [are SARMs legal?](/blog/are-sarms-legal-fda-wada-tga)

## What are the risks?

The DEA says the adverse effects depend on the user's age and sex, the steroid used, the amount used, and the duration of use. It lists:[^2]

- in adolescents, permanently stunted growth
- in women, permanent changes including a deeper voice and more facial and body hair
- in men, shrinking of the testicles, enlarged breast tissue and sterility
- liver damage and raised cholesterol
- aggression, hostility, psychological dependence and addiction
- withdrawal symptoms, including severe depression, when long-term use stops abruptly

NIDA warns that misuse "can lead to early heart attacks, strokes, liver tumors, kidney failure, and psychiatric problems", and that the doses people take when misusing steroids "can be 10 to 100 times higher than the doses used to treat medical conditions".[^1] The studies behind these warnings, including the heart and fertility research, are in [the health risks of anabolic steroids](/blog/anabolic-steroid-health-risks).
