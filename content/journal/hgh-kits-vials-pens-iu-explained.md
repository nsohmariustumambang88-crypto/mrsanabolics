Growth hormone is sold as a "100 IU kit", a "36 IU pen", a "10 mg cartridge" or a box of "10 x 10 IU" vials, and the labels mix international units and milligrams freely. This guide explains what somatropin is, how IU and mg convert, how the kit, vial and pen formats differ, and what the packaging of the three Beligas listings we have just added actually says.

It does not tell you whether to use growth hormone, how much, or what it will do. Those are medical questions, and the honest answer to them comes from a doctor, not a shop.

## What somatropin is

Somatropin is the name for human growth hormone made by recombinant DNA technology. The US label for Genotropin, a licensed somatropin medicine, describes it as "a human growth hormone produced by recombinant DNA technology in Escherichia coli", a protein of 191 amino acid residues with a molecular weight of 22,124 daltons.[^1] The European product information for Omnitrope, another licensed somatropin, gives the same origin: "produced in Escherichia coli by recombinant DNA technology".[^2] Licensed somatropin is a prescription medicine; the Genotropin label is marked "Rx only".[^1]

When a carton says "191 amino acid sequence" and "rDNA origin", as the Beltropin carton does, it is describing its contents in those terms. That is a statement printed on a box, not a test result.

## IU and milligrams

Somatropin is labelled in both units, and licensed labels state the ratio. Omnitrope's product information describes a cartridge of "5 mg somatropin (15 IU)", another of 10 mg (30 IU) and another of 15 mg (45 IU), and gives the 5 mg strength as "3.3 mg of somatropin (corresponding to 10 IU)" per mL.[^2] That is 3 IU for every milligram, so any label can be converted one way or the other:

| Labelled IU | Equivalent mg |
|---|---|
| 10 IU | 3.3 mg |
| 36 IU | 12 mg |
| 75 IU | 25 mg |
| 100 IU | 33.3 mg |

The ratio is also a quick check on a label. The Beltropin EZuse pen prints "36IU (12mg)/mL", which agrees with it. The Beligas 75 IU kit lists ten vials of 2.5 mg: 25 mg in all, which is 75 IU.

## Kits, vials and pens

### Powder vials, with or without water

One format is a box of vials of freeze-dried (lyophilised) powder. Some boxes add vials of bacteriostatic water, which makes them a "kit" in the fuller sense; others hold the powder vials alone. The box should say which, and how much is in each vial.

### Cartridges and pens

Licensed somatropin medicines such as Genotropin and Omnitrope come in cartridges used with a pen or a dedicated device. Genotropin is "a white, lyophilized powder" supplied in 5 mg or 12 mg two-chamber cartridges, "with the lyophilized powder in the front chamber and a diluent in the rear chamber", and in single-dose devices that also contain a two-chamber cartridge.[^1] Omnitrope is a ready-to-use solution in a glass cartridge, which its product information says "should only be administered with" the pen made for that presentation.[^2] A pen is therefore a device built around one particular cartridge, not a generic container.

### How licensed labels say to store it

Storage is part of what you are buying. The Genotropin label says to store the lyophilised powder "under refrigeration at 36°F to 46°F (2°C to 8°C) until the expiration date", in the package to protect it from light, and not to freeze it.[^1] Omnitrope's product information says the unopened cartridge should be stored and transported refrigerated at 2 °C to 8 °C, not frozen, and kept in the original package to protect it from light, and its carton adds "After first opening, use within 28 days."[^2] Any pack should carry its own storage instructions; if it does not, that is worth noticing.

## A printed strength is a claim, not a measurement

Every figure above is text on a box. A genuine pack carries a batch or lot number and an expiry date, and they match on the carton and on each vial or pen. Words such as "rDNA origin" or "Sterile" are part of the label, not evidence of what is inside it. The only way to know what a vial contains is a laboratory analysis of that batch.
